# Writing the companion

Write it the way a great editor would: an editorial-style explanation of the whole source in natural Egyptian Arabic, good enough that Mahmood can read it *instead of* the source.

- **Complete.** Every claim, the reasoning that links them, the examples that carry a point, every number, study and caveat. Drop greetings, sponsor reads and filler repetition. No target length: a dense talk may need more words than a long chat.
- **Egyptian, not فصحى.** «مش، ده، اللي، عشان، إزاي». Technical terms in standard Arabic with the English original once: «تكلفة الفرصة البديلة (Opportunity cost)», then one stable name.
- **Organised around the argument**, not the timestamps: the source's question, its answer, the steps between them. Each `##` unit is one idea with a heading that says the idea.
- **Faithful.** Quote exactly with `>` and explain after. Keep each claim's strength (observation vs. evidence vs. opinion). Never invent a number, study, quote or name; anything you add goes in `:::added`. Religious texts keep their wording, attribution and any grading the source gives.
- **Visual where it helps.** Use a comparison, steps, table, bars or timeline when the source really compares, orders or counts something, and explain it in the prose around it. Boxes are for the one thing to hold on to, not for every paragraph.
- **No filler.** No «في عالمنا المتسارع», «دعنا نغوص», generic moral endings or cheerleading; start with substance and end each unit when its point lands.
