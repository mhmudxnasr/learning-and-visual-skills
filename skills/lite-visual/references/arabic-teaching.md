# Teach the source in natural Egyptian Arabic

Read this before writing or revising companion prose. Use the complete accepted source. These are writing principles, not a reusable article outline or a mandatory review procedure.

## The reader and the job

Mahmood is an intelligent adult who wants an explanation he can comfortably read and understand without returning to the source for missing reasoning. Assume unfamiliarity with this source's specialist concepts unless his relevant competence is explicit. Explain the missing prerequisite where it matters; do not restart an entire introductory course or flatten a difficult argument into a slogan.

Before drafting, identify the source's central question, its actual answer, the steps connecting them, and the places a reader could misunderstand. Decide which definitions, examples, contrasts, or intermediate steps would resolve those difficulties. Use that understanding directly in the article; no separate brief, ledger, or review form is required.

Read the complete accepted source. A title, transcript fragment, previous companion, or generated overview is insufficient. For a source too long for one context window, read consecutive bounded portions and retain their exact boundaries, argument continuity, terminology, and unresolved references in the existing workspace. Return to the source when drafting the corresponding explanation.

## The voice

Use fluent written Egyptian Arabic: direct, thoughtful, precise, and comfortable over many pages. Use natural forms such as «ده»، «عشان»، «لما»، and «مش» where they belong. Keep familiar technical nouns in their standard Arabic form. Avoid phonetic exaggeration, excessive slang, repeated «بص يا سيدي», cheerleading, patronizing reassurance, or mechanically replacing a few formal words with colloquial ones.

Introduce a technical term with its Arabic meaning and original English term when useful, then use one stable name. English terms, code, equations, and identifiers get their own LTR isolation. Do not switch entire paragraphs into English or alternate Arabic synonyms for a single concept merely for variety.

Preserve direct quotations, scripture, hadith, legal wording, and source-defined technical language in their original register. Explain them in Egyptian Arabic outside the quotation. Handle transcription artifacts without making the reader study the transcription process: smooth ordinary errors in paraphrase, but never silently repair an uncertain quotation and attribute the repair to the speaker. Prefer an intact quoted excerpt plus faithful paraphrase when a defective word is not essential; if uncertainty changes the meaning, flag it briefly at that passage. Do not add spelling commentary for every transcription typo. Distinguish a translated quotation from a paraphrase. Never invent an attribution, source location, study, quotation, number, or result. Preserve an uncertain source's uncertainty; external factual additions require separately verified provenance and a visible editorial label.

Prefer concrete subjects and verbs: who did what, what changed, why, and under which conditions. Resolve «ده» and «هو» when two possible antecedents are nearby. Break a sentence when it contains several logical steps, but keep those steps connected with the actual relation: cause, consequence, exception, contrast, or evidence. Use Arabic punctuation naturally. Keep diacritics when they disambiguate a term or belong to an exact quotation.

Avoid canned openings and empty transitions such as «في عالمنا المتسارع»، «دعنا نغوص»، «من الجدير بالذكر»، and «وهنا تكمن الأهمية». Do not ban a useful word mechanically; replace a sentence when it contributes no specific meaning. Do not repeat the same point as an opening promise, a callout, a paragraph, a takeaway, and a conclusion.

## Explain the reasoning

At each difficult point, answer the reader's next real question. The following are teaching moves to select where needed, not mandatory subsections or a repeated card pattern:

- Give a usable meaning before relying on a label. Explain what a concept changes in the case at hand.
- Unpack a mechanism into connected steps. Naming the cause and outcome does not explain the connection between them.
- Bring the source's concrete example next to the claim it demonstrates. Explain which detail matters and how it supports the claim.
- Where notation matters, name every symbol, keep units and conditions, and work through the source's calculation without inventing a result. Clearly label any added toy example.
- Identify the tempting wrong inference when the source makes a subtle distinction. State the correct boundary without turning every section into a mandatory “misconception” box.
- Show how evidence supports the conclusion and how far that support reaches. Preserve the difference between observation, interpretation, correlation, causal evidence, hypothesis, recommendation, and value judgment.
- End a section when its reasoning has landed. Connect to the next section through the source's developing question; do not append a generic motivational takeaway.

Use the source's own examples first. An extra example is useful when it removes a specific obstacle; label it «مثال توضيحي مضاف» and keep it compatible with the source. An analogy must name the relationship it illustrates and, when needed, where the analogy stops. Added explanation may clarify the source but may not silently strengthen its claims.

Choose depth by conceptual difficulty and source detail. Do not target a fixed word count, compression ratio, number of sections, or number of visual aids. Short explanations can be excellent; a complex mechanism may need several connected paragraphs. Repetition, transcript padding, and an appendix cannot compensate for missing explanation.

## Match the material

An argumentative chapter needs the question, reasoning, examples, objections, and qualifications in their proper relation. A study needs the actual research question, participants/material, method, comparison, results, and stated limitations when the source supplies them. Do not fill missing fields from imagination. A procedure needs prerequisites, the real order of actions, expected outcomes, and source-supported failure cases. A technical explanation needs precise terms, mechanisms, and code or equations where essential. A lecture needs its developed argument with spoken repetition edited carefully, while preserving repeated passages that add a distinction or example. Narrative material needs its events, perspectives, and ambiguity; do not invent lessons or spoil material outside the assigned chapter. Religious material requires exact attribution and careful separation of quoted text, the scholar's explanation, and editorial clarification; do not independently derive a ruling.

## Writing examples

These miniature examples demonstrate writing decisions. They are not factual source material to insert into companions.

### Replace a label with an explanation

Weak: «التخزين المؤقت آلية محورية لتعزيز كفاءة النظام وتحسين تجربة المستخدم.»

Better: «التخزين المؤقت (Caching) معناه إن النظام يحتفظ بنسخة من نتيجة حسبها قبل كده. لما نفس الطلب يتكرر، يقدر يرجّع النسخة دي بدل ما يعيد الحساب. ده بيوفّر وقت، لكن لازم نحدد إمتى النسخة تبقى قديمة ونحسب النتيجة من جديد.»

The revision explains the action, the reason for the benefit, and the tradeoff. In a real companion, retain only claims supported by the source or explicitly label the extra explanation.

### Preserve the limit of evidence

Weak: «أثبتت الدراسة إن الأداة حسّنت الإنتاجية.»

Better, for a hypothetical source reporting only an association: «الدراسة لقت إن الناس اللي استخدموا الأداة أنجزوا أكتر. لكن النتيجة دي لوحدها ما تثبتش إن الأداة هي السبب؛ ممكن المستخدمين دول كانوا مختلفين من البداية. عشان كده، استنتاج الدراسة هنا ارتباط بين الاستخدام والإنجاز، والسبب لسه محتاج دليل.»

Do not add this caveat automatically to a source with a different design. Read what the actual study can establish.

### Make an example do explanatory work

Weak: «تكلفة الفرصة البديلة مهمة في اتخاذ القرارات، زي الاختيار بين الدراسة والشغل.»

Better, as an explicitly added example: «مثال توضيحي مضاف: لو عندك ساعتين وقررت تقضيهم في الدراسة، مش هتقدر تقضي نفس الساعتين في شغل مدفوع. العائد اللي كان ممكن تكسبه من أفضل بديل متاح هو تكلفة الفرصة البديلة (Opportunity cost). المقارنة هنا بين اختيارك وأفضل بديل ليه، مش مجموع كل الحاجات اللي ما عملتهاش.»

The example identifies the constrained resource and the distinction the term captures. Do not present it as the author's example.

### Repair a sentence without erasing a qualification

Weak: «يُفضي تباين السياقات إلى تقويض قابلية تعميم النتائج على نحو مطلق.»

Better: «النتيجة دي ظهرت في الظروف اللي اتعملت فيها التجربة. لو الظروف اتغيّرت، ما نقدرش نفترض إن نفس النتيجة هتتكرر من غير دليل.»

The revision preserves the restriction and makes its consequence understandable. Do not replace precise source conditions with this generic wording when the source names them.

## Write and revise directly

Apply fidelity, teaching, language, and continuity while writing. Fix an omitted qualification, unexplained term, stiff sentence, or broken transition when you encounter it. Keep numbers and attributions accurate, examples connected to their claims, terminology consistent, and the ending complete.

Mahmood removed the mandatory four-pass review, meaning-unit forms, anchored review ledger, and authored-text review digest from ordinary production. Do not replace them with another ritual or four full rewrites. Return to the relevant source passage when uncertain and revise the affected prose. Do not manufacture corrections or reports to demonstrate effort.

Excellent explanation remains the goal. The integrity receipt makes no claim that the prose was reviewed or that every important point was mechanically verified.
